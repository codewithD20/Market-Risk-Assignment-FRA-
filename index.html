    The question

    We called one portfolio aggressive and one safe. Would that call have survived a crash?

    Two portfolios of ten stocks each, no stock shared, in each. We send both back to a calm market and to the COVID crash, measure what they would have lost, and then ask an optimizer whether it would have chosen our weights in either era.

    Step 2 · Time-travel test #1

    Did “high risk” behave like high risk back then?

    We pick one calm stretch and one crisis, hold today's weights, and compute Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall for both portfolios in both windows. VaR is the monthly loss you should expect to exceed only 1 time in 20 (or 1 in 100). Expected Shortfall is the average loss once you are past that line.

    Step 2, continued · Extra measures

    VaR gives the size of a bad month. It does not say why, or how long.

    Three more lenses close that gap. Beta shows how much of each move is borrowed from the market. Maximum drawdown shows the full peak-to-trough fall an investor sat through, not a single month. The CAPM split shows whether risk came from the market or from the stocks themselves, which tells you whether diversification could have helped.

    Step 3 · Time-travel test #2

    Would an optimizer in each era still pick our weights?

    Take the return each portfolio earns on its full record, October 2017 to March 2026. Rebuild the Markowitz efficient frontier from each era's own returns and ask for the lowest-variance mix that hits that same return, with every stock held at 1% or more. Then compare the answer with the weights we actually hold.

    The verdict

    The risk label travels through time. The “optimal” weights do not.

    A risk call based on beta and volatility would have been right in a calm market and right in a crash. The weights are a different matter: they depend on which years you feed the optimizer.

    How to read these results with care

      Method

      • Data. Month-end closing prices, September 2017 to March 2026 (102 monthly returns), ten stocks per portfolio plus the Nifty 50.
      • Portfolio return. Weighted sum of stock returns with the fixed weights shown in Step 1.
      • Historical VaR. The 5th or 1st percentile of the window's monthly returns (linear interpolation, the same rule as Excel's PERCENTILE.INC).
      • Parametric VaR. Normal distribution with the window's mean and the standard deviation from the variance-covariance matrix.
      • Expected Shortfall. Average of returns at or below VaR (historical), or the normal tail mean (parametric).
      • CAPM. OLS of portfolio excess return on Nifty excess return; risk-free 6.5% a year.
      • Ratios. Sharpe = (annualised return less risk-free) / annualised volatility. Treynor = the same excess return / beta. Information ratio = annualised alpha / annualised residual volatility.
      • Frontier. Minimise portfolio variance subject to weights summing to 100%, each weight at least 1%, and expected return equal to the target.
      VaRparam = μ + zα·σ  ·  σ² = wᵀΣw  ·  EShist = mean(r | r ≤ VaR)
